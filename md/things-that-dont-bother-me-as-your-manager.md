# Things That Don't Bother Me as Your Manager


### 1. Leaving a meeting that isn't useful to you
If you're not needed, and you're not getting anything from it, take the time back. There are plenty of other meetings, I promise 📆

### 2. Disagreeing with me publicly
If you think I'm wrong, please say so. I'd much rather be proven wrong quickly than confidently head in the wrong direction ➡️

### 3. Not having topics for our 1:1
It's your time, not a status meeting. Wanna talk about the show you're watching or the funny thing your dog did? Awesome! Or, if you prefer, take the 30 minutes back. We'll chat the following week 📝

### 4. Taking last-minute time off
Life doesn't always give us two weeks' notice. Take the time you need and I'll worry about the deliverables 🗺️

### 5. How you give me feedback
You don't need to find the perfect words. If you trust me enough to tell me something I could do better, I'm already grateful 🥹

### 6. Not responding to my Slack right away
My message doesn't become urgent just because it came from your manager. Finish what you're doing, I'm happy to wait 🕥

### 7. Not sitting at your desk for 40 hours a week
Good work doesn't only happen at a desk. Sometimes the best thinking happens on a walk, over coffee, or when you're technically done for the day. Take that time back ☕️

### 8. Telling me no
An opportunity isn't an obligation. If you don't have the bandwidth, or just don't want to do something, tell me. I'd rather have an honest no than a reluctant yes 🫡

### 9. Eating during a meeting
Please eat. Nobody does their best thinking hungry, and snacks make most meetings better anyway. Just please bring me some too 🌯

### 10. Asking "Why are we doing this?"
Please ask. You deserve to understand the purpose behind your work. And if I haven't provided context proactively, that's on me 🙋‍♀️

### 11. Having an unproductive day
Some days are just… not the day. One unproductive Tuesday doesn't undo all the great work you've done ☑️

### 12. Telling me you're interviewing for another role
Of course I'd love for great people to stay on my team. But your career belongs to you, not me. If another opportunity gets you closer to where you want to go, I'll support you 🤗

### 13. Disagreeing with feedback I give you
Feedback is perspective, not fact. Listen, reflect, keep what's useful, and feel free to disagree with the rest 🤔
